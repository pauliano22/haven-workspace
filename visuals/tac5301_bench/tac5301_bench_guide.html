TAC5301 bench test: at-the-bench guide

One chip, a microphone, a speaker and the dev kit (DK) on a breadboard. Written 14 Nov 2026 from the chip's datasheet. Nothing here has been run on hardware yet. This test is not needed for the v3 pod.

The one thing that is easy to miss: the chip's main ground is the metal square under its belly. The adapter has no pad for it, only a hole. Solder it through the hole from the back and run a wire from it to ground, or the chip will not answer.

1. Before the trip: do you have these?

Ticks are saved on this device only.

2. Solder the chip to the adapter

  1. Use the 24-pin position (marked 4×4 mm). The chip sits turned 45°, on the X of silver fingers. The fingers already carry solder.
  2. Find pin 1. Chip: the corner with the dot. Adapter: header hole "1". Beep from hole 1 to find its finger. Pins count counter-clockwise seen from above.
  3. Turn a spare chip over and beep between the centre square and the four corner contacts. They should all beep together. If not, stop and tell Claude.
  4. Flux the fingers, place the chip, check all four sides with a magnifier, tape one side.
  5. Belly pad first: flip the board, flux in the hole, iron inside the hole against the chip's metal, feed thin solder for about 3 seconds. Add a wire to that solder: it goes to ground.
  6. Each finger: iron tip in the groove at the outer end, slide toward the chip until it touches. Do not press down. Flux each side first.
  7. Look for bridges between neighbouring fingers. Fix with flux and solder wick.
  8. Meter, no power: neighbouring header holes must not beep together. Holes 10 and 12 are both ground and may beep with each other; hole 11 between them must not beep with either.
  9. Solder in the header pins and push the adapter across the breadboard's centre gap.

3. Wire it (power off, battery unplugged)

Header hole numbers are chip pin numbers. Ground means the breadboard's ground rail, joined to a GND pin on the DK and the battery's black wire.

  • Microphone: the pin joined to the metal can is ground. The other is +.
  • DK VDD is 3.0 V, the bottom edge of what the chip accepts (3.0 to 3.6 V). If the chip answers but behaves oddly, feed pins 11 and 23 from a real 3.3 V source (the Teensy's 3.3 V pin, grounds joined).
  • Keep microphone and speaker about 30 cm apart, or they howl.
  • The 1 µF capacitors on pins 13 and 14 must be rated 25 V or more.

4. Power-up order

  1. Beep: 9 V rail to ground must not beep. VDD rail to ground must not beep.
  2. Plug in the DK, switch it on. Pins 6 and 11 to ground: about 3.0 V.
  3. Touch the chip. Warm means unplug and re-check pins 10 to 13 and the belly pad.
  4. Clip in the 9 V battery last. Pin 13 to ground: about 9 V.

5. Run the test

Hand the DK to WSL from Windows PowerShell: usbipd.exe list, then usbipd.exe attach --wsl --busid <the J-Link line's id>. Then in the WSL terminal:

~/projects/active/haven_workspace/firmware/haven_zephyr_app/tools/tac5301_bench.sh --build

After moving a wire, re-check without re-loading:

~/projects/active/haven_workspace/firmware/haven_zephyr_app/tools/tac5301_bench.sh --check-only

Do not connect the Haven app during this test.

If the first chip will not answer after an hour of checking, build the second adapter instead of reworking the first.

6. Once it passes

  • Speak and tap: does sound pass cleanly? Hiss and hum on a breadboard do not count.
  • Current, if the meter has a mA range: in series with the wire to pins 11 and 23. The datasheet says about 24.5 mA.
  • The delay measurement needs an oscilloscope with two probes, or a stereo line input. Tell Claude which you have.
  • Send: the printed lines, photos of the adapter (both sides) and the breadboard, and what the corner-contact beep test showed.

Nothing in this test goes in or near an ear.
